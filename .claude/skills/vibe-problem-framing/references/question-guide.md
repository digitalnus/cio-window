# Question Guide

One round, maximum eight questions. Then write the statement with whatever comes back, including nothing.

Eight is a ceiling, not a target. Ask only what closes a real gap: a question already answered in the prompt, or whose answer wouldn't change the statement, is friction that costs the answers that were needed. A detailed prompt earns two questions; a bare one earns most of them. If nothing material is missing, skip the round.

---

## The questions

| # | What is needed | Ask roughly like this | Format |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Who is affected | "Who has the worst time because of this?" | Single |
| 2 | What goes wrong | "What actually goes wrong? Tick everything you've seen." | Multi |
| 3 | What it costs | "When that happens, what does it cost you?" | Multi |
| 4 | How big it is | "Roughly how often does this happen?" | Single |
| 5 | Why now | "Why fix this now rather than last year?" | Single |
| 6 | The cause | "Which one of these is causing most of it?" | Single |
| 7 | What's not included | "Which of these are you *not* trying to fix right now?" | Multi |
| 8 | What they already have | "Do you already have anything that shows this is happening?" | Multi |

The numbering is the order to ask them in, not a list to trim from the bottom. Questions 1 and 3 matter most: without the first there is no statement, without the third there is no reason to build anything.

---

## Questions that need care

- **Q3 (cost)**: Push past restatements. "People wait longer" is the problem again, not its cost. Offer options that land somewhere real: hours lost, work redone, money spent twice, decisions delayed, people giving up.
- **Q4 (how big)**: Where fabrication starts. Offer ranges people recognise — "a handful a month", "a few a week", "several a day" — plus "honestly, no idea". Never write a number that didn't come from an answer.
- **Q5 (why now)**: Something changed; something went wrong and got noticed; it's been like this for years; a deadline is coming. Add "nothing in particular" — not a failed answer, it tells about urgency.
- **Q7 (what's not included)**: Offer nearby *problems*, never features. Add "nothing — all of this is part of it".

Q6 always includes "not sure which is biggest".

---

## Writing the questions and options

- Short question, everyday words, no product vocabulary.
- 4-5 options, concrete and distinguishable. If two could describe the same day at work, merge them.
- Options describe things that happen to people, never features.
- Every question gets an escape: "Other — none of these fit", plus an honest-ignorance option wherever a made-up answer would poison the statement.
- On Q1 the escape is "not sure who has it worst" — never "all of them".
- Scope the problem, never the build. Q7 asks which nearby problems sit outside the statement.
- Only ask what they can answer from their own experience.
- Never ask about anything already in the prompt.

---

## When several boxes are ticked

- **Frictions**: put the one with the most impact in the statement; the rest go in the one-line note beneath.
- **Costs**: combine them. Two or three named costs in a row is exactly the shape.
- **Causes**, if "not sure": build around the most likely one and make ranking them the first suggestion.
- **If frictions and costs belong to a different group than Q1**: keep the statement on the group they named and note the rest beneath.
